Mechanical barcodes for cells

MBARC develops microlaser-based acoustic sensors that identify cells and particles one by one by their mechanical signature, without chemical labels.

Each microlaser emits its own spectrum. Pick a cell to see its code.

What MBARC is

Today, tracking individual cells within a sample requires labels that alter them or that blur together. MBARC proposes another route: using each cell's mechanical properties as its identity, read with laser light at the micrometre scale.

Microlasers

Tiny resonators that emit light at very precise wavelengths, inside or next to the cell.

Acoustic sensing

The cell's mechanical vibrations shift the laser spectrum and leave a measurable fingerprint.

Barcode

That fingerprint becomes a unique code that lets you identify and follow each cell or particle.

How it works

The process has four stages, from sample to code.

  1. Add the microlaser

    Cells or particles are coupled to microlasers that act as probes.

  2. Excite with light

    An external optical pulse triggers laser emission and its spectrum is collected.

  3. Record the mechanical response

    The cell's acoustic oscillations modulate the spectrum in a characteristic way.

  4. Generate the code

    Spectral peaks are translated into a barcode that identifies each element.
